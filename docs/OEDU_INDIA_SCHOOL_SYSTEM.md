# The Indian school system, and what OEdu has to be for it

Written 2026-10-17. Source: NITI Aayog, *School Education System in India: Temporal Analysis and Policy Roadmap for Quality Enhancement* (Education Division, May 2026), 232 pages, read in full except the four scanned foreword pages (ii–v), which have no text layer. Page numbers below are the report's printed numbers.

Two things this note is, and one it is not.

- It is what the report says about how an Indian school is built and run, boiled down to what OEdu must model.
- It is an audit of where OEdu today treats **every** school as an American high school, with the evidence.
- It is **not** a specification for report cards, board exams, fees or timetables. The report does not contain them. It is a policy analysis (UDISE+ 2014-15 to 2024-25, PARAKH 2024, NAS 2017 and 2021, ASER 2024) with 33 recommendations. Where this note needs a board's rule, it says so and says whose it is to supply.

## 1. What the report says an Indian school is

**Scale.** 14.71 lakh schools, 24.69 crore students, 1.01 crore teachers (p.14). 68% of schools are government, 5.4% government-aided, 23% private unaided, 2.7% "others" (madrasas, schools run by religious or charitable bodies, unrecognised private schools, alternative centres) (p.16). Government schools hold 49% of the children, private unaided 39%.

**Structure.** NEP 2020 replaces 10+2 with **5+3+3+4**, ages 3–18 (p.7): Foundational (three years of pre-school or Anganwadi, then Grades 1–2), Preparatory (3–5), Middle (6–8), Secondary (9–12). The statistics use five levels: pre-primary, primary 1–5, upper primary 6–8, secondary 9–10, higher secondary 11–12. The report uses Grade, Class and Std (Standard) interchangeably; the SFHS workbook says Class, and the India screens already follow it.

**A school covers a range, not the whole ladder.** Table 3.2 (p.17) sorts schools by the grades they offer: 1–5, 1–8, 6–8, 1–10, 6–10, 9–10, 1–12, 6–12, 9–12, 11–12. Half are primary-only; **5.4% offer 1–12**. So a child changes school at Grade 5, 8 and 10, and those are where they are lost: transition 5→6 is 92.2%, 8→9 86.6%, 10→11 75.1% (Table 3.4, p.29); dropout is 0.3% in primary, 3.5% in upper primary, 11.5% in secondary (p.36). The report's remedy is "composite" schools (1–10, better 1–12) and **school complexes**: one secondary school with the schools below it and the nearby Anganwadis, within 5–10 km, sharing specialist teachers, labs and monitoring (p.140). OEdu's "campus" model (one school, one building) is the exception here, not the rule.

**Who may be a student, and what is owed to them.**
- The Right to Education Act, 2009: free and compulsory education for ages 6–14; **25% of seats in private schools reserved** for economically weaker and disadvantaged children (p.5); a pupil-teacher ratio of 30:1 (primary) and 35:1 (upper primary); 200 working days and 800 instructional hours (primary), 220 days and 1,000 hours (upper primary) (pp.105, 120). Grade 9 onward is outside the Act, so fees, books, uniforms and transport become the family's (p.25, p.117).
- Social categories the school records and reports on: **SC, ST, OBC, General ("Others")**, plus minorities, girls and transgender students, and the umbrella "socio-economically disadvantaged groups" (SEDG) (pp.7, 116).
- **Children with Special Needs (CwSN)** under the RPwD Act 2016: early identification, an **Individualised Education Plan**, special educators, assistive devices, accessible buildings (p.67); screening for learning disabilities (the report names the PRASHAST tool); a cluster-level Inclusive Education Resource Person (pp.171–172).
- **Migrant** children: a migrant-status field in the admission register, mid-year admission and class re-adjustment, inter-state transfer protocols (pp.118, 169).
- Re-entry: bridge courses, open schooling through NIOS, recognition of prior learning (p.169).

**Language.** Medium of instruction the mother tongue or home language at least to Grade 5, preferably 8 and beyond; the three-language formula kept with flexibility; Indian Sign Language and multilingual, accessible content (pp.7, 176).

**Identity and records the system already keeps.** UDISE+ (school and, since 2022-23, **student-wise records with unique student IDs**) (p.21); SDMIS; the Child Tracking System; **APAAR ID** (used to follow a child from age 3 to 8) and the Poshan Tracker for Anganwadis (p.191); an HR system for teachers. A recommended **early-warning flag** for an "at-risk student" in the school register, built from attendance, learning level and basic socio-economic vulnerability (p.168). Per-block counts of who moved 5→6, 8→9 and 10→11 (p.169). Schools without connectivity "depend on manual systems" to feed these, and the data is often not used (pp.115, 119).

**People.** Teachers: a four-year integrated B.Ed as the minimum; eligibility tests (TET/CTET); national professional standards (NPST); continuing development through NISHTHA and DIKSHA; contract and vocational-trainer posts alongside regular ones (pp.7, 120–122). School heads: induction, a leadership competency framework, autonomy over calendar, grants and local programmes (pp.152–153). Others the report names as roles in a school: well-being nodal teacher, two Health and Wellness Ambassadors per school, physical-education instructor, librarian, counsellor, Anganwadi worker and helper, school-community worker (pp.180–184, 169). Single-teacher schools are 1.04 lakh (p.112).

**Governance above the school.** Centre (Department of School Education & Literacy) → State (directorate, SCERT, State School Standards Authority, State Board) → District (DEO, DIET, a district task force chaired by the District Magistrate) → Block (BEO, BRC/BRPs) → Cluster (CRC/CRPs) → School. At the school: the head and a **School Management Committee** (also called SDMC, VEC, VSS, VKS, JBVS by state), mandated by RTE to oversee the school and write a **School Development Plan**, with parents and women represented; they keep minutes, a grievance register and publicly displayed funds, enrolment, attendance and infrastructure (pp.157–159).

**What a school's own facts are measured by** (the UDISE+ infrastructure indicators the report tracks): functional electricity; drinking water; handwash; separate boys' and girls' toilets; **CwSN-friendly toilets**; ramps (with handrails); library; science laboratory; computers; internet; smart classrooms; boundary wall; menstrual-hygiene provision (pp.42–54, 114–115).

## 2. How learning is judged

- **National surveys, never individual grades.** NAS (Grades 3, 5, 8; 2017, 2021) and PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024 (**Grades 3, 6, 9**, i.e. the end of the Foundational, Preparatory and Middle stages) report against **competency codes** with a description and a national average, e.g. "C-8.4 arranges numbers up to 99…" (Table 3.9), "C-1.2 represents and compares commonly used fractions…" (29%, Table 3.12). NAS "benchmarks … without grading individual learners" (p.79). ASER is household-based, rural, ages 3–16 (p.73).
- **Subjects as the report names them by stage.** Grade 3: Language, Mathematics. Grade 6: Language, Mathematics, **The World Around Us** (which the NCF has put in place of EVS in Grades 3–5) (p.91). Grade 9: Language, Mathematics, Science, Social Science (pp.96–98).
- **What the report wants inside a school.** A baseline learning assessment at the start of the year; a daily "foundational hour"; grouping by **level rather than by grade** (Teaching at the Right Level, the Graded Learning Programme); formative, competency-based classroom assessment with a **minimum 30% application and reasoning questions** in school summative tests (p.178); feedback conversations; a state bank of validated assessments; a **Holistic Progress Card** that carries physical-fitness and global-citizenship indicators (pp.182, 185); a simple per-competency report card parents can read (the Rajasthan example, p.196). Foundational literacy and numeracy benchmarks for Grades 1–5, extended to 8.
- **Wellbeing as an outcome.** An annual fitness assessment for students and teachers; a school happiness and well-being index from student self-reports; exam-stress sessions around board examinations (pp.182–183).
- **Vocational and career.** Vocational exposure from Grade 6 (NEP 16.4), aligned to NSQF levels, with credits that travel (National Credit Framework, Academic Bank of Credits), "bagless days", career counselling from Grade 8 (pp.186–188).
- **Early years.** Anganwadi and Balvatika as the first three years; school-readiness reports passed to the Grade 1 teacher; two transition events a year (pp.190–192).
- **AI.** From October 2025, AI and computational thinking from Grade 3; the report asks for national and state guidelines on ethics, data privacy and authorship, and for "cognitive-safety" norms because over-reliance weakens attention and independent thinking (pp.124–125, 195). This is why the assistant built for the console is staff-facing and asks first, and why it is left empty here.

**Not in the report, and not guessed here:** how a school turns marks into a grade, what a CBSE, CISCE or State Board report card looks like, the academic year's dates (UDISE years are written 2024-25, which implies April–March; the report does not say), term structure, fee rules, how a transfer certificate is issued. These come from the school's board and are the SFHS workbook's to supply.

## 3. Where OEdu treats every school as an American high school

The product was built from one US online high school's rules and from a US student-information system's feature list. The only thing keyed on a school's region anywhere in the code is `adminTree()` (`learn/app.js`), which shows an extra **India** group to a school whose `organizations.region` is `IN`. Everything else is drawn for everyone. So an Indian school gets a US console with an India shelf bolted on. The other direction does not leak (a US school never sees India), which is why it has not been noticed.

What is already right for India, and must stay: the `region` field; Classes (X-C: class teacher, room, strength); one typed row per child with admission number, roll, house, category, APAAR (12 digits, REFUSED, NOGEN), PEN, consent and both parents' +91 mobiles; the Compliance page; the age cut-off set to 1 April; people import with guardians. They are on the **India** shelf only, which is itself the symptom: they are not the school's own structure, they sit beside it.

## 4. The fix: a school is its own profile

The rule: **nothing is global except the engine.** A school's shape is data on the school, and the code asks the school, never assumes.

A **school profile** is chosen when the school is made and can be edited by its administrator. It carries:

| Part | US (generic) | India | Today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Region | `US` | `IN` | built (`organizations.region`) |
| Structure | Pre-K … 12; "Grade" | Pre-primary, Class I … XII; stages Foundational / Preparatory / Middle / Secondary; a **coverage range** (a school may be 1–8 or 9–12) | grade list is US only |
| Calendar | fall–spring, quarters or semesters | April–March, Term 1 / Term 2, periodic tests | quarter / semester / year only |
| Time and number | school's own zone, MM/DD/YYYY, 1,000,000, $ | school's own zone, DD/MM/YYYY, 10,00,000 (lakh, crore), ₹ | New York for all |
| Grading | letter scale, GPA, credits where a school wants them | percentage, grade bands set by the board, competency tags, no GPA | one US scale |
| Records | state student ID | admission number, APAAR, PEN, category, CwSN, migrant status, medium of instruction, mother tongue | India shelf only |
| Attendance | per-period, ADA | class register, % and the 75% flag | per-period |
| Places in the console | what a US office uses | what an Indian office uses | one tree; India filtered in |
| Words | Grade, GPA, counselor | Class, marks, class teacher | US words everywhere |
| Rules a named school brings | e.g. EHS's diploma | its board's | EHS's diploma for all |

Two consequences of "each school is its own":

1. **EHS is a school, not a country.** Its diploma rules, 4.0 scale and Regents belong to *its* organisation (`graduation` as a school-level module that is on for EHS and absent elsewhere), not to the US default.
2. **A place is shown because the school has the thing,** not because the person is signed in. The same test the API already applies for permission is applied for profile: no screen, no endpoint and no default for something the school does not use.

### Order of work

0. **Stop the leak (small, reversible).** Tag each console place and family tile with the regions it is for; hide the EHS/US-only ones from an Indian school (graduation, transcripts, GPA, state reporting, meal eligibility; the student Transcript and Graduation; the parent Graduation Tracker and College and Career) and refuse their addresses. No data change, the API is untouched, and a US school sees exactly what it saw. *Done in this change* in `learn/app.js` (`forRegion`, `regionOf`, `not: ["IN"]` on GPA, Transcripts, Graduation, Meal Eligibility, State Reporting, the student Transcript and Graduation) and `learn/parent/parent.js` (the Graduation Tracker and College and Career tiles). Checked on the local SFHS demo school (an Indian administrator, student and parent) and a US school: the Indian rail has no Record heading, an address like `/student/Graduation` lands on Overview, and the US school is unchanged. **Not covered:** the phone shell (`phone-record.js`, `phone-student.js` still draw Credits, Transcript and Graduation; desktop first), the API (it still answers those routes for any school), and the places that are US in *wording* rather than in kind (support-plan types, the family view's "Counselor" and "Pronouns" facts, "Grade" for a class).
1. **Locale and time.** `organizations.timezone`, a school locale; reminders, "today" and dates read them; DD/MM/YYYY, lakh/crore, ₹ for `IN`. Needs a migration.
2. **Structure and calendar.** A school's grade list and stages, its coverage range; a named academic year (April–March for `IN`); terms (Term 1 / Term 2) beside quarter / semester.
3. **Grading.** Named, reusable scales per school (percentage, board grade bands entered by the school); marks that can carry a **competency code**; theory/internal split; the Holistic Progress Card for the Foundational and Preparatory stages. The bands themselves come from the school's board, not from this note.
4. **Class register and attendance %.**
5. **Records the report names:** social category set (SC/ST/OBC/General/EWS), CwSN with an IEP (not 504), migrant status, mother tongue and medium, early-warning flag, transition tracking (5→6, 8→9, 10→11).
6. **School-level modules:** SMC and School Development Plan (minutes, grievance register), school health screening with referral, mid-day meal, UDISE+ facts page and return.
7. **Content.** Courses authored by the school against its own board's syllabus; no US catalogue shown to a school that has not chosen it.

Not to do: take the report's national averages as a school's targets; write a board's grade bands or file layouts from memory; build fees (held by your ruling; the readiness note still lists it first for an Indian office).

## 5. Decisions only you can make

1. **Which boards first?** CBSE, CISCE (ICSE/ISC) and the State Board each want different report cards and files; step 3 cannot be written until one is named and its layout supplied.
2. **Is EHS a named profile** (its own module set) **or just "US"?** This note assumes the first.
3. **Hide or remove?** Step 0 hides and refuses by region; nothing is deleted, so EHS is untouched. Say if you want the US code taken out of the India build entirely.
4. **Who writes the Indian default vocabulary** (Class, marks, class teacher, term) — you, or the SFHS office from their own forms.
