## Implementation plan

### 1. Goal and the test of success

Goal: a CBSE or CISCE private school of about 1,800 children and 60-75 staff can run its whole year on OEdu and retire the incumbent ERP and its WhatsApp-group workarounds. The owner's own words for what stops that today: "Without those [fees, a family app, HPC and the timetable], no Indian school can switch to it fully."

Proposed test of success (the owner has not stated one): one named school runs one full term, April to the Term-1 report cards, on OEdu alone. Register, admissions, fee collection, circulars to parents, report cards including the Holistic Progress Card, certificates, and the board and UDISE+ lists, with no parallel spreadsheet.

No source in the repo or the brief names a pilot school, a paying customer, a price, or who inside a school decides to buy. The only school in the data is the demo, Strawberry Fields High School (1,801 children, 68 sections, 59 teachers). Whether any real school uses OEdu today is not stated anywhere and is the first open question (section 11).

### 3. Scope

In this plan:

- Workstream A, the UI/UX rebuild of the whole front end `learn/` to the brief, ten steps (section 4).
- Workstream B, the capability gaps in section 2.2 that the rebuild alone does not deliver (section 5).
- Workstream C, cross-cutting rules that every step obeys: data protection as the school's processor, Hindi and regional languages, performance budgets, audit on every change (section 7).

Not in this plan unless the owner says so: changing the backend stack (Cloudflare Worker + D1 behind Routes → Services → Repository), anything that deploys to production or changes live data, exam domain logic (owned by the Exams brief), AI marking of papers from photos, biometric and RFID hardware, live GPS hardware, board and UDISE+ upload formats the school or board has not supplied, SFTP (the brief deletes it).

### 4. Workstream A: the UI/UX rebuild (the brief's ten steps)

Why it exists: a live audit on 26/10/2026 found 72 screens built as 37 different layouts, 45 ranked UX problems, an 18-item menu, a search that finds menu items but no student, a narrow centred modal for every create and edit, US vocabulary in an Indian school, no top bar, a sign-out that hangs, and no parent portal. The brief replaces patching with one system: 11-item rail, 56 px top bar with entity search, tokens, 9 page templates T1-T9, 33 components C-01..C-33, an India glossary and status dictionary, URLs as the source of truth for filters, tabs and drawers.

Technical rules (locked): vanilla JavaScript on the current codebase; `learn/api.js` is the only file that touches the network; raw colours and sizes only in `tokens.css`; no strings in pages (they come from `OL.i18n`); no native `<select>` outside `ui/select.js`; no `confirm()` or `alert()`; ICU plurals; dd/mm/yyyy, ₹ with lakh grouping, "Class X-C". A page file declares config and the template renders it.

Definition of done for every screen (brief §9.4), applied before merge: (1) declares one template and uses only library components; (2) H1 equals the menu label and the breadcrumb equals rail group › tab; (3) one primary action with a dictionary verb; (4) search, server pagination, filters with counts, empty, filtered-empty, loading, error and no-permission states; (5) dates, numbers and money from the format module, India labels from the locale pack, no vendor names in copy; (6) the right create/edit surface, with no form of more than four fields in a centred modal; (7) works at 360 px, keyboard-only, axe clean in light and dark; (8) URL reflects filters, tab and drawer; (9) visual-regression baseline and a UI smoke test; (10) an audit event for every change shown in the Activity tab.

Performance budgets: JS per route ≤ 120 KB gzip (shell ≤ 60 KB); CSS ≤ 40 KB; ≤ 2 font files; LCP ≤ 2.0 s on throttled 4G Moto-G class; INP ≤ 200 ms; CLS ≤ 0.05; lists paginate server-side and never send more than 100 rows except exports.

### 5. Workstream B: capability gaps

Each item is a thing a school cannot switch without. All sit behind a module flag until built (the brief: "not built = not in the menu").

- **B1 Family app.** Separate phone-first PWA shell at `/family` (never the admin with fewer menu items): sign-in by mobile number with a WhatsApp or SMS one-time code, 90-day sessions, "Is this you?" identity confirmation, child switcher, Home, Attendance, Homework, Results, Fees, Circulars with acknowledgement, Calendar, Certificate request, Profile and DPDP consent, Language; offline cache of the last Home, Attendance, Homework, Results and Circulars; installable. Unknown numbers never create an account and never reveal whether a child exists; five codes per hour per number. Needs a sender for the codes (B5).
- **B2 Fees.** Fee heads, plans per class set and year, instalments with due dates and grace, student fee account (plan + transport slab + concessions − payments), concessions with approval (sibling, staff ward, RTE 25%, merit, EWS), collection by UPI, cash, cheque and card, dues and reminders, receipts with immutable numbers (a cancelled receipt keeps its number and appears flagged in exports), accounting export. Rule already built and kept: nothing about a certificate or a result reads anything about money. Needs the owner's payment-provider account.
- **B3 Holistic Progress Card.** Finish what v1 started: the child's own screen, the official stage layouts (foundational, preparatory, middle, secondary), parent input, competency evidence, and a printed card. Human approves any AI-drafted remark (B10).
- **B4 Timetable and homework.** Rebuild the grid on T8 with clash display by class, teacher and room; draft versus published timetable; a homework diary (teacher sets, family reads) and lesson plans.
- **B5 Channels.** WhatsApp Business, SMS under DLT templates, app push, email; message delivery state; fallback order WhatsApp → SMS → push; never email-only for families. Needs a WhatsApp Business account and a registered SMS sender (about ₹5,900), both owner actions.
- **B6 People.** Staff attendance, payroll with PF, ESI and TDS, leave balances, the 50-hour training log, recruitment.
- **B7 Campus.** Transport with routes, stops, riders and live location with parent alerts; inventory and store; canteen; hostel; visitor gate pass.
- **B8 Care and compliance.** POCSO reporting flow, SQAAF self-review, consent centre with history, export and erasure requests and a read-access trail; upload files for UDISE+, CBSE and CISCE once the school supplies the layouts.
- **B9 Core.** School groups with several branches and a group-level view; Hindi and regional languages across the admin console, reviewed by a native reader.
- **B10 AI helper.** Drafts circulars and HPC remarks and flags at-risk children; every output is a draft a person approves. Needs a model key and the AI side panel from another branch.

### 7. Cross-cutting rules (Workstream C)

- Data protection: the school is the controller and OEdu its processor, never an owner of the data. Children's data needs verifiable parental consent, purpose limits, and a record of who read it. APAAR and Aadhaar stay optional and masked (Aadhaar never kept past the last four). Phone numbers are masked in lists; a reveal is an audited action.
- Audit: every change fires `module.entity.verb` and shows in the record's Activity tab. Marks and registers keep their lock-and-flag rule.
- Languages: en-IN is the base, en-US only where the school's country is US, hi-IN at launch of the family portal, Punjabi as built; Devanagari font loads only for Hindi.
- Phone: teacher must-haves (Today, Register, My timetable, Homework, Marks entry, Circulars) and the whole family portal work at 360 px.
- Seeded data at real size (1,801 students, 68 sections) so performance and pagination are tested at school scale.

### 8. Dependencies only the owner can unblock

### 9. Risks (the author's list)

1. Scale: ten rebuild steps across 72 screens plus eight layers of capability is more than one builder delivers soon; the rebuild produces no new customer-visible capability for several steps.
2. No demand evidence: no named pilot, so the order of work rests on the owner's map and an audit by a demo user, not on a school's behaviour.
3. Two frames: the strangler shell shows legacy pages in the old style beside rebuilt ones for as long as the rebuild lasts.
4. Money: fees mean immutable receipt numbers, reconciliation, refunds, provider failures and a statutory trail; a mistake costs a school real rupees.
5. Children's data and consent law; a sender that mis-delivers an OTP exposes a family.
6. Conflicting status between sources (section 2.2) means some of the "gaps" may already exist, or the live site may be behind the repo.
7. Hindi quality without a native reader.
8. Specifications not public: upload layouts, official HPC layouts, DLT template rules.
